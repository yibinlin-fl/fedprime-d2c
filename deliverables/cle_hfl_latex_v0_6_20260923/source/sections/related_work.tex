\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Model-Heterogeneous Federated Learning}

Model heterogeneity prevents direct parameter averaging across clients with different architectures. FedDF uses ensemble distillation over unlabeled data \citep{lin2020feddf}. Prototype-based methods communicate class representations: FedProto aggregates client prototypes, whereas FedTGP learns trainable global prototypes with an adaptive-margin contrastive server objective \citep{tan2022fedproto,zhang2024fedtgp}. RHFL addresses noisy labels with confidence-weighted collaboration \citep{fang2022rhfl}. AugHFL studies common corruption under model heterogeneity, and RAHFL extends this line with diversity-enhanced local representation learning and asymmetric collaboration \citep{fang2023aughfl,fang2025rahfl}. Our setting keeps model heterogeneity but asks whether client-specific class--corruption associations create directional class cues and whether those cues arise mainly during local learning or collaboration.

\subsection{Corruption Robustness and Consistency Learning}

CIFAR-C and ImageNet-C established common-corruption evaluation across noise, blur, weather, and digital distortions \citep{hendrycks2019corruptions}. AugMix combines stochastic augmentations with Jensen--Shannon consistency to improve corruption robustness and uncertainty \citep{cubuk2020augmix}. CLE-HFL changes the statistical structure: corruption operators are associated with task classes during client training. The diagnostic therefore compares different operators for the same semantic source rather than only enforcing consistency around one corrupted observation.

\subsection{Spurious Correlation, Group Robustness, and Environment Inference}

Shortcut learning and group distribution shift are well studied \citep{geirhos2020shortcut}. GroupDRO optimizes worst-group risk with known groups \citep{sagawa2020groupdro}; GEORGE recovers hidden subclasses before group-robust training \citep{sohoni2020george}; EIIL infers environments without a predefined taxonomy \citep{creager2021eiil}; and JTT upweights examples misclassified by an initial ERM model \citep{liu2021jtt}. CVaR and related DRO objectives emphasize upper-tail loss \citep{rockafellar2000cvar,duchi2021uniform}. In our setting, PEW supplies a taxonomy-assisted corruption-family proxy, and BER changes the effective risk mass assigned to class--pseudo-environment supports; pseudo-group discovery and group reweighting themselves are established ideas. Our CVaR-DRO arm is a protocol-matched empirical tail-risk control rather than a line-by-line reproduction of either cited formulation.

\subsection{Federated Spurious Learning and Counterfactual Diagnosis}

Federated spurious features have direct precedents. Personalized federated learning can exploit client-specific spurious features \citep{wang2024pflspurious}; FedPIN targets shortcut-averse personalized invariant learning \citep{tang2024fedpin}; and FedCD studies spurious correlation in federated domain generalization \citep{ma2024fedcd}. The current citation audit treats FedCD as an arXiv preprint. Counterfactual shortcut evaluation also appears in other domains, including medical imaging \citep{vigneshwaran2026counterfactual,jabbour2020chestxray}. We specialize this idea to paired interventions that change only the corruption operator for a fixed source, score the response along a pre-registered client binding, and pair that diagnostic with local-versus-federated formation analysis and a local mitigation objective.
